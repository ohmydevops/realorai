## What makes a portrait feel photographic

A believable portrait is a consistent scene, not a checklist of pores and defects. The person's anatomy, pose, surroundings, illumination, camera position, focus, and image processing should agree. A slightly soft face in an ordinary setting can feel more photographic than a perfectly detailed face floating in incompatible lighting.

Before writing a prompt, decide:

1. Who is present and what ordinary activity explains the moment?
2. What real-world place supports that activity?
3. Where is the camera, and how far is it from the person?
4. Is this a DSLR photograph, a phone photograph, or another user-requested capture style?
5. What light is actually available in that place?
6. Which parts should be in focus, and which limitations naturally follow?

Choose a small number of compatible details. Do not pile every photographic imperfection into one image. Real photographs can be clean, sharp, and well exposed; forced dirt, blur, noise, or flaws can become another artificial style.

## Real-world places

Place the subject somewhere that could exist and supports the action. Useful settings include an apartment kitchen, a neighborhood sidewalk, a bus shelter, a modest cafe, a public park, a workshop, a library reading area, a residential courtyard, a market, or a marked walking trail.

Give the environment enough physical structure to explain the photograph:

- A cafe has tables at usable heights, chairs with legroom, an entrance, and plausible walking space.
- A bus stop has a shelter or sign aligned with a road and pavement; the person waits where someone could stand safely.
- A kitchen has a usable counter, believable cabinet proportions, and utensils related to the action.
- A workshop has a work surface and tools placed within reach rather than decorative machinery fused into the background.
- A park has paths, planting, benches, and ground that fit the local climate and maintenance level.

Use ordinary wear selectively: a scuffed chair, a folded coat, a stained mug, a worn handrail, or a slightly uneven pavement. Avoid turning every surface into a distressed texture. The place should feel occupied, not staged with a prop in every empty space.

### Named locations

When a particular real location is requested, use reliable references for its architecture, materials, climate, street furniture, and spatial arrangement. If references are unavailable, choose a generic plausible setting rather than asserting landmark accuracy. Avoid inventing a recognizable building facade or exact address and presenting it as verified.

For an Iranian setting, for example, make local details coherent with the requested city and season; do not scatter unrelated regional motifs as decoration. Visible writing should be plausible for the place. If exact readable signs are unnecessary, keep them distant, naturally obscured, or outside the frame instead of relying on prominent invented lettering.

An AI image of a real place remains an AI interpretation. A location name in the prompt is not evidence that someone was photographed there.

## Natural poses and physical support

Give the person something simple to do. Waiting, listening, holding a cup, resting after a walk, reading, adjusting a sleeve, leaning lightly on a railing, or sitting with a bag beside them usually provides a clearer physical situation than an instruction to look candid.

Start with the body's support and weight:

- Standing: feet contact the ground; the hips and torso balance over the supporting leg or legs. A relaxed stance may place more weight on one side.
- Sitting: the pelvis rests on the seat, the torso fits the posture, and legs have room beneath or beside furniture.
- Leaning: a hand, shoulder, forearm, or back contacts a surface that could support the pose.
- Walking: the stride, body lean, arm position, and contact with the ground describe the same moment.
- Holding an object: the grip follows the object's shape, size, weight, and orientation.

Natural asymmetry helps when it follows the action: one shoulder slightly raised by a bag strap, a head turned toward a speaker, a sleeve pushed back on one arm, or one hand resting while the other works. Do not make every limb asymmetric merely to avoid symmetry.

Avoid unsupported back arches, twisted wrists, extreme neck rotations, elbows intersecting furniture, floating shoes, and hands positioned without an understandable task. A relaxed person does not need to look disheveled or uncomfortable.

### Hands and contact

If hands are visible, keep their job uncomplicated. Inspect the number and attachment of visible fingers, the position of the thumb, the wrist angle, and whether the grip could hold the object. Occluded fingers should disappear behind the object logically. Do not require every finger to be visible.

Check contact shadows and pressure: a mug should sit in the hand or on the table, a forearm should rest on the railing rather than hover, and a bag strap should lie against clothing. Clothing and skin may compress slightly at contact points.

Do not crop every portrait to hide hands as a substitute for making a coherent scene. Crop naturally when the intended portrait calls for it.

## Camera position, perspective, and scale

Specify the camera's physical position before adding lens language. State whether the photographer is standing or seated, whether the camera is near eye level or slightly above or below, approximately how far away the subject is, and whether the view is frontal or at a gentle angle.

Perspective follows camera position and subject distance. A lens name alone does not repair an impossible viewpoint. Do not request a very close wide-angle selfie while also demanding the facial proportions of a distant portrait.

Use these practical starting points as visual directions, not mandatory exposure recipes:

| Capture situation                | Plausible starting point                                                                            | Expected appearance                                                                                       |
| -------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| DSLR head-and-shoulders portrait | Camera near eye level, a comfortable distance, roughly 50–85 mm full-frame-equivalent field of view | Moderate perspective, some background separation, facial features without extreme near-camera enlargement |
| DSLR environmental portrait      | A few steps back, roughly 35–50 mm equivalent, context intentionally included                       | Person and surroundings share a clear spatial relationship; background may remain recognizable            |
| Phone rear-camera portrait       | Main camera, comfortable conversational distance, ordinary handheld framing                         | Wider environmental context, substantial scene detail, restrained phone processing                        |
| Phone selfie                     | Camera within arm's reach, slightly elevated if appropriate                                         | Nearer features may appear larger; supporting arm and viewing direction agree with the camera position    |
| Seated cafe portrait             | Photographer seated or standing at a stated height across or beside the table                       | Table edges, chair heights, eye line, and object sizes follow that viewpoint                              |

### Perspective checks

- People and objects decrease in apparent size with distance in a coherent way.
- Table edges, floor joints, windows, and building lines suggest compatible vanishing directions.
- The camera angle explains which tops, sides, and undersides of objects are visible.
- A hand close to the lens can look larger, but its connection to the arm must still make sense.
- The horizon and eye level fit the camera height; do not force every line or head to sit on the horizon.
- Reflections in windows, mirrors, or glasses should fit the scene and viewpoint rather than introduce a second incompatible composition.

Slight handheld tilt is acceptable when the scene remains coherent. Avoid using warped walls or slanted furniture as a shortcut to a spontaneous-looking picture.

## Face, expression, and gaze

### Project acceptance standard: face first

The user rejected the cafe portrait `ai-collection-20261017-32` despite its plausible pose and setting: its facial rendering, expression, and lighting still felt visibly synthetic. Treat this as a stricter acceptance standard for future people images. A plausible background, natural-looking hands, or a prompt containing DSLR does not make the face acceptable.

Before accepting any portrait, inspect the face at the actual game size and at a larger view. Check the relationship between eyelids, eyeballs, brows, nose, cheeks, mouth, jaw, and ears. Look for a fixed glossy gaze, over-defined eyes, sculpted cheek shading, oddly compressed mouth corners, repeated fine texture, and facial sharpness that does not match the rest of the photograph. Describe observed defects rather than declaring an image universally indistinguishable from a camera photograph.

Judge the expression as a moment, not a display of facial detail. Do not default to a friendly closed-mouth smile, tilted head, and direct eye contact in every portrait. Use a neutral resting expression or a small action appropriate to the scene when that produces a more credible moment. Keep individuality and subtle asymmetry without manufacturing deformities or exaggerated skin flaws.

Review the face and scene together: the same light direction, color environment, contrast, and camera processing should apply to both. Reject an otherwise ordinary room whose person looks separately lit, composited, beauty-retouched, or substantially sharper than nearby objects at the same depth. Avoid routinely combining a bright cool window on the face with glowing amber background fixtures and carefully sculpted shadows. That combination can be physically plausible, but it did not meet this user's preferred everyday appearance in the rejected example. Prefer simpler, uneventful illumination unless the user asks for another style.

When the face feels synthetic, correct its structure, expression, tonal transitions, or capture situation first. Extra pores, grain, blur, lower resolution, and generic negative prompt lists are not substitutes. Do not claim that camera-model names or added imperfections guarantee realism. If a revision still fails this standard, report the limitation instead of approving it automatically or starting an unrequested generation batch.

Describe a specific modest expression: listening attentively, a small uneven smile, a neutral resting face, mild concentration, or squinting slightly in bright outdoor light. Avoid requesting a perfect smile, flawless beauty, or an intensely emotional expression without a situation that explains it.

Keep facial structure consistent across the head. Small differences between the two sides are ordinary; grossly misaligned eyes, detached ears, melted nostrils, or disconnected teeth are structural errors.

The gaze needs a target: the lens, a person beside the photographer, a book, a work surface, or something off-frame. Both eyes should support the same plausible viewing direction. Avoid an empty fixed stare when the rest of the body implies engaged activity.

For visible teeth, favor a believable mouth shape and natural variation. Do not demand that each tooth be separately sharp or perfectly aligned. Lips, gums, cheeks, and jaw should support the expression.

## Skin without the plastic look

Preserve variations in tone and texture that suit the person's age, features, light, and viewing distance. Useful details can include subtle pores, fine facial hair, mild redness, smile lines, uneven lip texture, or a few ordinary blemishes. None is mandatory for every person.

- Texture should vary across the face; cheeks, eyelids, lips, and nose do not share one repeated pattern.
- Highlights should follow the light and skin surface without making the entire face oily or metallic.
- Fine detail should be strongest where resolution, focus, and lighting support it.
- Avoid beauty-filter smoothing, porcelain skin, repetitive pore stamps, uniformly sharpened wrinkles, and invented scars.
- Preserve the requested complexion. Do not brighten skin automatically or use stereotypes to define realism.

At game display size, normal skin may look relatively smooth. Do not exaggerate pores simply to make realism visible in a thumbnail. A credible face depends on shape, light, and expression as well as texture.

## Eyes, hair, clothing, and accessories

### Eyes

Eye surfaces can have reflections, but catchlights should be compatible with the visible or implied light sources. Avoid identical decorative white dots pasted onto both eyes. Eyelids should wrap the eyes; eyelashes should grow from the lid edges rather than form thick symmetrical fans by default.

Glasses should sit on the nose and ears with plausible perspective, frame thickness, and reflections. They may obscure part of the eyes. Do not make lenses perfectly invisible while also showing strong reflections elsewhere.

### Hair

Use a coherent hairline, directional growth, groups of strands, and a few flyaways where appropriate. Avoid a solid helmet edge, repeated curls, uniformly luminous strands, or hair that merges into ears, collars, and background objects. Facial hair should grow from skin and vary naturally in density.

### Clothing

Choose everyday clothing suitable for the place, weather, activity, and person's stated preferences. Fabric should fold around joints, drape under gravity, and compress where the body rests on a surface. Seams, collars, buttons, pockets, and sleeves should connect logically.

Avoid adding logos, jewelry, layered accessories, or elaborate patterns merely to increase detail. When accessories are present, check their scale, attachment, and interactions with hair and clothing. Respect the requested dress and cultural context without turning it into costume.

## Light that belongs to the place

Describe a concrete source: a large window to one side, overcast daylight, open shade beneath a tree, direct afternoon sun, ordinary ceiling lights, or a mixture of street lighting and shop windows. Let the lighting explain the face and environment together.

Check direction, softness, intensity, and color:

- Window light should fall from the window's side; room surfaces may supply weaker fill.
- Overcast outdoor light often gives soft transitions without erasing all shadows.
- Direct sunlight can produce stronger shadows and squinting; do not demand perfectly open eyes and flat illumination in the same situation without explanation.
- Warm indoor bulbs and cooler daylight may create modest color differences across the scene.
- Contact shadows connect the person to nearby surfaces.

Avoid unexplained rim lights, perfect facial fill in a dark room, glowing skin, evenly bright deep shadows, or a studio lighting arrangement hidden inside an ordinary snapshot prompt. Do not add sunset light automatically; many convincing photographs are made in uneventful midday or indoor light.

## DSLR character

Use DSLR-style capture when the requested portrait benefits from intentional focus and framing. A plausible default is a normal or short-telephoto view, focus around the nearer eye, moderate background separation, and restrained processing. Select aperture language only if it supports the scene; extreme blur is not required.

Focus should occupy a plausible region of the scene. A face turned away from the camera may put the farther eye a little softer. If shallow depth of field is requested, do not also demand every ear, hand, nearby object, and distant sign be equally crisp.

Background blur should reflect distance and scene depth, not a uniform blur pasted behind a cutout person. Keep fine hair and translucent edges believable. Avoid exaggerated creamy bokeh, excessive microcontrast, and razor-sharp detail over every surface as default realism cues.

## Mobile-phone character

Favor an ordinary rear-camera photograph unless the user requests a selfie or portrait mode. Include the surroundings naturally, use plausible handheld framing, and allow modest computational processing without heavy smoothing or aggressive HDR.

A phone photograph can have substantial depth of field and a recognizable background. Portrait-mode blur is optional, not a universal phone signature. When used, check boundaries around hair, glasses, fingers, chair backs, and gaps between limbs; avoid a cutout silhouette and depth that changes arbitrarily within one object.

In dimmer light, slight softness or fine noise may be appropriate. In bright daylight, a clean image is equally plausible. Avoid adding a timestamp, phone border, fake EXIF panel, or camera-brand watermark to prove the capture style.

## Unpolished does not mean damaged

Choose one or two incidental imperfections that follow from the situation: a slightly loose crop, a minor flyaway, an unarranged sleeve, faint low-light noise, a distracting but ordinary background object, or a brief expression between posed moments.

Avoid stacks of artificial grain, random motion blur, lens dirt, light leaks, chromatic fringes, crushed blacks, and compression blocks. These can obscure errors without making the underlying scene more believable. Do not degrade a finished image simply to conceal AI artifacts.

Keep color restrained and appropriate to the light. Avoid automatic teal-and-orange grading, oversaturated irises, bleach-white teeth, glowing foliage, and extreme local contrast. Natural color does not require a gray or desaturated image.

## Prompt construction

Write one coherent capture situation. Lead with person, action, and place; then camera position, light, focus, and finish. Use concrete instructions instead of long strings such as masterpiece, ultra-realistic, award-winning, 8K, cinematic, flawless, or perfect anatomy.

Suggested structure:

```text
Use case: photorealistic-natural.
Subject and action: an adult person doing a simple ordinary activity.
Place: a physically plausible real-world setting with a few relevant details.
Pose: clear body support, relaxed weight, understandable hand contact, and a specific gaze target.
Camera: DSLR or mobile phone; camera height, distance, angle, and framing.
Light: a concrete source consistent with the place and time.
Rendering: believable skin, fabric, perspective, focus, and restrained processing.
Finish: an ordinary unretouched photograph with a small number of natural imperfections.
Constraints: no beauty filter, plastic skin, theatrical lighting, exaggerated HDR, text overlays, or watermark.
```

Camera descriptions guide appearance; they do not create authentic capture provenance. Keep the asset's AI label and generation credit in the collection metadata.

## Review before accepting an image

Inspect both a normal viewing size and a closer view. Use the size shown in the game to judge the overall photograph; use the closer view to find anatomy, contact, and edge errors. Do not reject an otherwise suitable image solely for a detail that normal resolution could not resolve.

| Area             | Accept when                                                        | Repair or reject when                                                                  |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Place            | Layout, materials, objects, and activity form a plausible setting  | Architecture warps, furniture is unusable, or local details contradict one another     |
| Pose             | Weight, joints, grip, and support make sense                       | Limbs intersect, hands float, or the body cannot sustain the pose                      |
| Perspective      | Viewpoint, scale, and visible surfaces agree                       | A table, face, reflection, or background implies an incompatible camera                |
| Face             | Expression and gaze are coherent, with plausible natural variation | Features melt, duplicate, detach, or point in incompatible directions                  |
| Texture          | Skin, hair, and fabric respond to focus and illumination           | Plastic smoothing, repeated textures, harsh detail everywhere, or fused edges dominate |
| Light            | Sources explain shadows, highlights, and scene color               | Facial lighting contradicts the surroundings or objects lack contact shadows           |
| Camera rendering | Focus, blur, and processing fit the intended capture               | Cutout blur, unexplained sharpness, or excessive HDR creates a synthetic finish        |
| Provenance       | Real/AI label, credit, and source are honestly recorded            | Appearance is used as proof of authenticity or metadata is invented                    |

If one defect matters, describe and repair that defect while preserving the rest of the scene. For example: keep the cafe, expression, lighting, and framing; correct the hand so the fingers wrap around the mug handle. Do not regenerate a whole collection because one portrait has a bad grip.

For a fundamental contradiction, simplify the scene or change the viewpoint. Avoid trying to solve impossible geometry by requesting more detail or higher resolution. Follow the imagegen workflow for edits and preserve originals unless replacement is requested.
