Popular AI image generators: a beginner’s guide

AI image generators turn written descriptions into pictures, and many can edit existing images too. Here are seven well-known tools and model families to help you understand the landscape. This is an introduction, not a popularity ranking.

Last reviewed: October 18, 2026

ChatGPT Images

OpenAI’s image tool lets you create a picture by describing it in a chat, then ask for changes in follow-up messages. It also supports editing uploaded images.

Official guide: ChatGPT Images

Midjourney

Midjourney turns text prompts into images through its website or Discord. Its workflow lets you explore variations and refine a visual idea, from an illustration to a photograph-like scene.

Official guide: Midjourney

Google Gemini

Google’s Gemini app includes image generation and editing under the Nano Banana name. You can describe a scene, upload reference images, and request changes to the picture in conversation.

Official guide: Google Gemini

Adobe Firefly

Firefly is Adobe’s family of generative creative tools. Its text-to-image tools let you explore different styles and compositions. The Firefly platform also offers partner models, so check which model you selected.

Official guide: Adobe Firefly

FLUX

FLUX is a family of image models from Black Forest Labs. It supports image generation and editing, including workflows that use reference images. You can access supported models through the company’s playground, API, and other services.

Official guide: FLUX

Stable Diffusion

Stable Diffusion is a family of models from Stability AI. Alongside hosted services, supported models can run on your own hardware. This offers more control over the workflow but requires suitable hardware and setup; licenses vary by model.

Official guide: Stable Diffusion

Ideogram

Ideogram creates images from text and puts particular emphasis on typography, such as words inside posters, covers, and logos. Always proofread generated text, especially in languages or scripts a model handles less reliably.

Official guide: Ideogram

Which one should you try?

For conversation-based creation, explore ChatGPT Images or Gemini. For a workflow built around image variations, explore Midjourney. For Adobe tools, look at Firefly. For model-level control, investigate FLUX or Stable Diffusion. If your design needs text inside the picture, explore Ideogram and carefully check the spelling.

An app is the interface you use; a model is the engine behind it. One app may offer several models. Features, access, pricing, and usage terms change, so check the official documentation before choosing.

Can you tell a generated image from a photograph?

These tools can make convincing pictures, and a realistic appearance does not establish where an image came from. Try Real or AI? to practice looking closely at photographs and generated images.
