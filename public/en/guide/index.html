How to spot AI-generated photos

Some AI pictures look like ordinary camera photos at first glance. A closer look can reveal inconsistencies in how the scene fits together. These are clues, not a reliable yes-or-no test.

Physics and contact

Look at how objects connect. Does a hand actually hold the cup? Do the chair legs reach the floor? Can you follow a bag strap over the shoulder? Separate parts may look convincing while the connection between them makes little physical sense.

Light and shadows

Try to account for the light on the face, clothing and surroundings. Shadows under a chin or beside an object should make sense for the light source and nearby surfaces. Several lamps, a window or reflected light can produce different shadows, so different directions alone do not establish an error.

Glass and mirrors

Compare reflections with the scene. Can the objects and their positions be explained from the reflected viewpoint? A reflection is not a duplicate of the camera’s direct view. The mirror angle and curved glass can change what appears and how it looks.

Writing and repeating details

Enlarge signs, clothing text, logos and numbers. Look for letters merging or changing shape within a word. Follow repeated structures such as railings and buttons to see whether they remain consistent. Correct text does not rule out AI. Models can render it, and lettering can also be added later.

Faces, hands and edges

Inspect finger attachments, ears, teeth and the boundary between hair and clothing. Sometimes an edge merges with another object or a detail stops without an explanation. Natural faces are not symmetrical or flawless. Blur, makeup and camera processing can also make real features look unusual.

Distance, scale and focus

Compare object sizes with their distances. Table edges, walls and roads should fit the camera viewpoint. In portraits, look at the transition from hair to blurred background. A cutout-looking edge can also come from a phone’s portrait mode, so it is not specific to generated images.

What can the original tell you?

A higher-quality copy may reveal details hidden in a screenshot. Reverse image search can also find a creator’s post identifying the picture as AI-made. Missing camera metadata proves little, since sharing can remove it. AFP explains

How much can you trust an AI detector?

A detector’s percentage is not a guarantee. In its 2025 guide, Full Fact says it has not found these tools reliable for routine checks, instead using image analysis, source searches and expert input. A detector result alone does not establish authenticity. Full Fact’s guide

Creation and editing history

Some files carry Content Credentials with signed information about their origin or edits. C2PA explains that valid credentials do not establish that a claim attached to the image is true. Missing credentials do not establish that it is fake either. C2PA’s explanation

A real portrait and an AI variation

These portraits share a similar composition. The AI version uses the real photo as a reference, with a different person and a small change in expression. Looking photographic does not establish where an image came from.

As models improve, these mistakes can become less common or disappear. Their absence does not prove a photo is real, and one odd detail does not prove it is fake. For important pictures shared online, check the original source and caption too.
